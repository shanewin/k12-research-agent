# Subject Line Rules

## The Core Problem

Most edtech cold emails have subject lines that are LABELS, not teasers:
- "the product AI Reading Tutor for Your District" — this is a label
- "Following up after the conference" — this is a label
- "Improving Reading Proficiency with Technology" — this is a label

These get deleted without opening because they look like every other
vendor email in their inbox.

## The Fix: Write the Subject Line LAST

Write the email body first. Then find the most interesting, specific phrase
in the email and make THAT the subject line. Think of it like a movie
trailer — tease the best part, don't summarize the whole thing.

## Rules

1. TEASE, DON'T LABEL
   - YES: "Your structured literacy push"
   - YES: "Quick question about your improvement plan"
   - YES: "The gap between screening and practice"
   - NO: "the product Reading Tutor for Fresno Unified"
   - NO: "Improving ELA Proficiency in Your District"
   - NO: "AI-Powered Reading Intervention Solution"

2. KEEP IT SHORT (5-8 words)
   Short subject lines stand out in an inbox full of long ones.
   They also look more like a personal email from a colleague.

3. USE VISUAL IMAGERY OR CONTRASTING IDEAS
   These two techniques create curiosity:
   - Visual imagery: "Sunday nights and lesson plans" (paints a picture)
   - Contrasting ideas: "Great screeners, missing practice" (tension)

4. TOUCH REAL EMOTIONS (ETHICALLY)
   Educators feel stress, pressure, fatigue, urgency, hope, pride.
   A subject line that names what they're feeling gets opened.
   - YES: "The post-screener question nobody's answering"
   - YES: "When curriculum alone isn't enough"
   - NO: "Your students are falling behind" (guilt-tripping)
   - NO: "URGENT: Reading crisis in your district" (manipulative)

5. DON'T SOUND LIKE EVERY OTHER VENDOR
   Avoid the words and patterns every edtech email uses:
   - "innovative", "data-driven", "evidence-based", "transform"
   - "Introducing...", "Announcing...", "Meet..."
   - ALL CAPS, excessive punctuation, or emoji in subject lines
   If it sounds like it could come from any of the 50 edtech companies
   emailing this person this month, rewrite it.

6. FOLLOW-UP SUBJECT LINES
   Do NOT use "Following up" or "Checking in" as subject lines.
   Each email in the sequence should have its own standalone subject
   that would get opened even if the previous emails were never seen.
